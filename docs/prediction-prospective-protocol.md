# Prospective period-estimate evaluation — review draft

Prepared October 11, 2026; updated October 12. **Internal planning draft for optional future evaluation; not a frozen study protocol or completed validation.** The owner declined independent review; the [internal statistical review](prediction-statistical-review-2026-10-12.md) is complete. No new participant records have been collected. The [readiness record](../research/protocols/period-prospective-review-v1.json) lists decisions needed only if this study is pursued. External review is not a development or release gate.

## Question and intended scope

Evaluate the existing experimental median policy as it would actually be used: what date does it issue, how often does it withhold, and what information was available at that moment? The [completed comparisons](prediction-heldout-results.md) support retaining this reference; they do not establish acceptable accuracy for intended users. The weighted candidate is not being promoted or tuned further in this protocol.

Proposed first scope is an observational evaluation with consenting adults using the Android app. Entry history, age range, recruitment setting, life-stage categories and follow-up length need definition before enrollment. Include cold starts, unknown context and changing patterns in the availability accounting; do not recruit only people expected to receive a date. Participants for whom the policy withholds remain part of the cohort. Perimenopause-specific accuracy requires suitable representation and labels defined separately from prediction outputs; age alone cannot establish those labels.

The target is a participant-reported period onset supported by a pre-agreed diary and adjudication process, not a laboratory-verified physiological event. It is not an ovulation, fertility, contraception, hormone-level or treatment endpoint. Study participation and ordinary phone feature testing are separate activities.

## Baseline and separation from earlier work

Baseline is preview **0.18.0**, journal format **8**, repository revision `3258660`. The production policy's LF-normalized SHA-256 is `b048ee7833677b11f5f3aab102c92fd17e827eb363cfc77740862e12d6c9d268`. Its rules are six prior intervals within 365 days, supported historical lengths 14–90 days, maximum spread 14 days and maximum median backtest mean error 7 days, plus the existing context, completeness, observation, stale, expired and date checks. Those are preview product limits, not clinical definitions or study acceptance criteria.

Before collection, freeze the actual study build, complete implementation hashes, runtime, capture schema, endpoint definitions and analysis code. A study instrument could add local audit records but must preserve the reference policy unless a new protocol explicitly replaces it. The current app does **not** provide that audit instrument: `Entry` stores dated values, and estimate review is session state. A present-day backup cannot recover every earlier correction, review answer or issued prediction.

All Zenodo development, calibration and test participants have been examined. They may supply engineering examples but cannot become a fresh confirmatory cohort. Use new participants; record how overlap with previously examined sources is assessed and any uncertainty. A fixed model need not split a new validation cohort into development/calibration/test merely to reuse the old percentages. Complete pipeline debugging on fictional data or a separately consented feasibility cohort first; keep that cohort outside the confirmatory evaluation.

## Issuance and target rules proposed for review

1. Capture a deliberate completed estimate assessment after the user has finished its controls, rather than every UI render. Keep disabled/declined assessments separately from enabled requests. This event boundary requires a future instrument design; opening a panel is not equivalent to opting into research or requesting an estimate.
2. The primary assessment is the first enabled, completed request in an anchor episode. An episode begins when a newly reported start becomes the latest known start. Retrying, editing that start or restoring an older snapshot does not manufacture a new primary episode. Preserve repeated assessments as secondary records. Requests before any anchor exists remain in cold-start accounting, using one pre-anchor episode per participant.
3. Run the unchanged policy on the exact input snapshot at that request's actual time. Save every returned status, including unavailable results. Freeze an issued center date and historical range; later expiry, correction or a better-looking request never replaces it.
4. Label the next adjudicated onset after the **as-issued anchor**, using the outcome record locked at study end. Preserve the distinction between onset date, entry time and adjudication time. Do not substitute the next date merely entered into the app when an earlier unlogged onset is identified.
5. If the next onset had already happened by issuance but was logged late, retain it in as-issued error reporting when the anchor/target relation is adjudicable. Separately count these late forecasts and report the prespecified subset whose target was strictly after the issue date. Do not label the combined figure prospective future-event accuracy. Same-day cases are reported separately because a date alone cannot establish temporal order.
6. If the anchor was erroneous or an observation gap prevents identifying the first following onset, label the outcome unresolved with its reason. Keep the original forecast and denominators. An outcome assessor should resolve labels without seeing the forecast or its error; retain disputed labels and the adjudication trail.

These rules address failure modes discovered in the source audit. They remain proposals to finalize alongside the capture instrument, especially episode identity after corrections and treatment of already-occurred or uncertain outcomes. Freeze any amendments before new outcome inspection. A retrospective dataset without these time records can support another explicitly idealized replay, not this prospective design.

Device wall clocks can be wrong. Retain a monotonic event sequence alongside local and UTC timestamps; flag conflicting order or time-zone changes. Compute calendar-date errors using the frozen local-date convention, never fractional 24-hour differences across daylight saving. If raw dates are shifted for analysis, preserve all relative timings, coverage and time-zone semantics consistently and document the transformation.

For imported history, distinguish when it became available to this app from when the source says it was originally entered. Historical values available at enrollment may be valid input to future requests; they are not evidence that the app issued predictions in the past. Restored records cannot reset episode identity or count a forecast twice.

## Outcomes, missingness and analysis

The principal accuracy estimand is pooled mean absolute calendar-day error among primary issued forecasts with an adjudicable target. Report it beside full cohort/request flow and outcome ascertainment, never by itself. Signed error is predicted minus reference date. Preserve extreme errors without trimming; target length cannot retrospectively determine eligibility.

Use these named counts, with explicit numerators and denominators:

- For all completed requests, including repeats, define `A` as completed assessments including disabled/declined ones, `E` as enabled requests and `O` as dates offered. Report `E/A`, `O/E` and `O/A` as usage measures, with repeat counts. These are request-weighted and must not be described as participant adoption rates.
- For the primary first-enabled-request-per-episode analysis, define `E1` as those requests, `O1` as dates offered on them, and `S1` as their exact adjudicated targets. Report `O1/E1` beside primary accuracy; disabled requests stay in `A` without preventing a later first enabled request from entering `E1`. Publish participant counts with no assessments, no opt-ins, no dates and no scored dates. No denominator means unavailable, not zero. This study does not measure the probability an arbitrary person opens the app.
- Partition `O1` into exact adjudicated outcomes, interval-uncertain outcomes, unresolved/invalid-anchor outcomes and right-censored outcomes. Record reason and follow-up duration. Within the exact group, distinguish target before, on and after the issue date.
- Publish pooled and participant-equal mean absolute errors separately, median and nearest-rank P90 absolute error, signed error, maximum error, within ±1/±3/±7 days, issued lead time and people with no offered/scored forecasts. The future-target subset is a prespecified secondary result, not a replacement headline selected after seeing errors.
- Publish historical-spread coverage and width as descriptive measures. No nominal confidence label or probability-calibration claim is supported by the current min–max range.

Do not treat withdrawal, study end, pregnancy, a long gap or no new entry as a zero-error outcome or an arbitrarily long observed cycle. A known contextual change after issuance does not erase a prediction. Record it and the observable outcome status under the frozen adjudication rules. For an uncertain onset interval, retain bounds and report a separate error-bound sensitivity analysis; do not choose the date nearest the forecast. Without a justified bound, overall missing-outcome MAE remains unidentified. For within-3-day reporting, let `C` be exact targets within three days: show `C/S1` and conservative ascertainment envelopes `C/O1` to `(C + O1 - S1)/O1`, treating every non-exact case as a failure or success respectively. These envelopes expose missing information; they are not estimated clinical accuracy. Keep invalid-anchor counts explicit rather than implying that each has a well-defined target.

Proposed uncertainty analysis resamples whole participants with replacement, including participants without forecasts, preserving their requests and paired comparator predictions. Keep 2,000 replicates, xorshift32 seed `20261011`, type-7 2.5th/97.5th percentile endpoints and counts of undefined replicates for continuity with the prior analysis, subject to internal statistical planning before study freeze. Prespecify life-stage/context, history-length and same-day/delayed-entry summaries with unknown groups retained; small groups get counts and explicit uncertainty, not general accuracy claims. Final category cutoffs and small-cell reporting rules are unresolved.

Rounded mean, last interval and fixed 28 may be retained as descriptive, offline comparators on the exact same snapshots and offered cases. They do not get alternative eligibility rules or appear as live user advice. No new model-selection hypothesis is proposed. Any fitted replacement or calibrated range needs its own development and independent evaluation design.

## Precision, review and study lock

Do not infer a sufficient sample size from 1,989 repeated forecasts or from two successful phone testers. Before choosing a participant count, specify desired interval width for MAE and availability, plausible participant heterogeneity, forecasts per person, outcome ascertainment, dropout and key subgroup needs, then justify participant count and follow-up. A separately governed feasibility stage can estimate collection burden and missingness; its outcomes cannot later be relabeled untouched confirmation data. Study stopping is fixed by enrollment/follow-up rules, not a favorable interim error.

The [readiness record](../research/protocols/period-prospective-review-v1.json) deliberately leaves recruitment count, follow-up duration, clinically meaningful error limits, availability/ascertainment limits and subgroup precision unset. Resolve them with the owner through internal statistical planning and intended-user input, with rationale, before confirmatory collection or access. A blank is an open decision, not a permissive threshold. An approved analysis plan and participant protections do not themselves establish product readiness.

Project controls for future collection: separate informed research consent, voluntary withdrawal, a named data custodian, minimal local encrypted records and an explicit export/retention/deletion plan. The ordinary app remains local-first. This draft neither enables telemetry nor creates hosting. The ignored `research/private-data` directory is not encrypted storage and is not automatically an approved destination for prospective or restricted records. The study owner and appropriate institutional reviewers determine applicable study oversight before recruitment. No provider contact, agreement acceptance or participant enrollment has occurred.

Before freezing, run fictional scenarios for late entry, backdated corrections, deleted starts, restored histories, missing context, context changes, long genuine intervals, observation gaps, study-end censoring, uncertain dates, duplicate requests, clock changes and withdrawal. Verify the emitted snapshot reproduces the issued result and future edits cannot alter it. A new local capture feature, export controls and any visible design must be specified and reviewed separately before implementation.

## Methodological sources

It is used here as a reporting reference, not as certification of this calendar rule or a claim of checklist compliance. The concrete event/episode rules above are project proposals; neither source prescribes them or supplies this study's sample size or clinical thresholds.
