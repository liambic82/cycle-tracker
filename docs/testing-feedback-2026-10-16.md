Removed copies and deleted entries suppress automatic reimport of the same source/id; Undo or explicit manual reimport clears suppression. A newly issued source ID remains a new identity and may need review. Restore turns automation off; actual Health Connect grants remain device-local.
- The optional hormone guide starts collapsed and includes **estrogen, progesterone and testosterone**, labeled as schematic educational patterns with separate relative scales. It has no personal marker, lab units or measured/predicted hormone values. Individual variation and the medical-advice limitation remain explicit. No prediction method, research plan, source records or frozen results were changed.

## Findings in 0.19.0

`App.tsx` owns a main `ScrollView`; `src/ui/Calendar.tsx` adds a fixed-height nested `ScrollView`. The calendar starts at the selected month with three loaded months, exposes a Load earlier months button, and resets its window/scroll position when the selected date changes. These are plausible contributors to the reported awkward scrolling; native frame timing and touch behavior have not been measured in this review.

`src/ui/DayEditor.tsx` fixes medications, sleep and daily observations before the symptom section, and uses a fixed `QUICK_SYMPTOMS` list. Both customization requests require preference persistence/migration and interaction checks, not just moving labels.

`src/ui/SleepImportSettings.tsx` already supports one transaction for several selected nights, but still requires load/select/review/apply each time. The Android adapter reads Health Connect only on request. `src/data/useJournal.ts` clears the vault key and journal references on session end. A locked background worker cannot simply call the existing save path without a different encryption/queue design.

## Recommended sleep automation design

The new question reopens automation planning; it does not silently enable a connection or supersede the owner's earlier deferral of closed-app syncing. Recommend automatic **foreground** import as the next step, alongside manual mode. It needs no Cycle Tracker hosting or account service.

1. Default off. User grants sleep access and selects the source once. Enabling explains that new nonconflicting sessions from that source will be saved without individual review. No automatic permission prompt on unlock; permission loss pauses import and offers a clear recovery action.
2. On successful unlock, while active, run one bounded recent-window read with an in-flight guard and cooldown. Reuse stable source/id deduplication and captured timezone rules. A recent-window lookback catches delayed records; older backfills continue through the manual history flow. Never switch providers just because another returns data.
3. Add new non-overlapping sessions in one validated transaction. Already-imported records are skipped. Corrections and overlaps, including ambiguous records within the same result, are queued for explicit review. Do not infer sleep duration from incomplete stages, overwrite manual values, sum naps, or silently mirror source deletions.
4. Show last successful check, new-session count, pending-review count and clear states for no new data, unavailable/denied access and source not yet synced. Mark imported only after encrypted persistence succeeds. Provide Check now and Saved nights; failure must not block manual journal use.
5. Lock/background/disconnect invalidates pending results. No plaintext staging, retained decryption key or background decryption is added. Restore/disconnect resets automation opt-in; restored preferences do not grant device access. Prevent unwanted reimport of a user-removed source record with an explicit suppression/restore policy.
6. Verify duplicate and stale results, failures/size limits, rapid unlocks, concurrent manual edits, corrected waking dates, travel/DST, new source IDs, missing stages, removal/suppression, restore, permission changes and lifecycle races. Validate actual Fitbit Inspire 3 behavior on the paired phone separately from synthetic checks.

Android supports foreground reads and optional background reads, with an additional permission and a runtime feature-availability check for the latter. Background operation is technically feasible, but requires a separate scheduling and encrypted-staging proposal before implementation; exact intervals and device support must not be promised. [Android read-data guide](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/read-data) · [HealthPermission reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/health/connect/client/permission/HealthPermission).

Google documents sleep-session and sleep-stage sharing through Google Health/Health Connect. Automatic reading by Cycle Tracker cannot make the tracker/source app publish a missing night; source freshness and connection state must remain visible. [Google Health sharing guide](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/14506680?hl=en). Sources checked October 16, 2026.

## Hormone chart scope

Offer an optional **Typical ovulatory pattern** illustration of relative estrogen/progesterone/testosterone changes. It has no lab units, personalized values, fertile-window claim, ovulation-date claim or “you are here” marker placed on the curve. Recorded cycle day remains a separate fact above it. Label it **Illustration, not your hormone levels** and retain the medical-advice qualification. The mock's lines are deliberately schematic, not fitted or sampled data.

Hormonal patterns and ovulation timing vary, including across cycles; an ovulatory template may not apply with hormonal contraception, perimenopause, irregular or non-ovulatory cycles. Calendar dates and wearable sleep do not establish an individual's hormone concentrations. The visual should not say where this person's hormones “should” be. [Office on Women's Health: Your menstrual cycle](https://womenshealth.gov/menstrual-cycle/your-menstrual-cycle). This is an assistant-led content/design constraint, not an external-review gate.

The user referred to Lively as an example, but no exact product link or screenshot was supplied. This proposal addresses the described concept; it does not claim to reproduce a verified Lively screen. Existing period-estimate research, frozen results and disclaimer remain unchanged.

## Testosterone evidence and wording

A 2024 study of salivary testosterone found a small aggregate mid-cycle peak and luteal decrease. A 2013 daily-serum study found higher mid-cycle testosterone on average but no discernible peak in every individual; day-to-day variation exceeded the cyclical component. These support the cautious phrase “may rise modestly around mid-cycle,” not a personal prediction or a universal hormone target. The schematic is hand-authored, not sampled/fitted study data; no absolute concentrations or between-hormone magnitudes are implied.
