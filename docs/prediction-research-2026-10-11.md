# Prediction research — October 11, 2026

## Decision

Prioritize **next period-start forecasting**. Keep the current experimental median as the reference method while evaluating alternatives outside the app. Treat personal ovulation/phase estimation as a separate research track requiring suitable measurements and reference labels. The current sleep import is insufficient evidence for implementing that feature.

This initial pass completes an evidence review, dataset-access assessment, evaluation protocol and reproducible synthetic evaluation of the **full production policy**. It does not establish population accuracy, validate predictions for perimenopause, or change preview 0.18.0. No participant records were downloaded during this initial pass, no data agreements were accepted, and no provider was contacted. The research code is not imported by the app.

The [same-day follow-up](prediction-benchmark.md) adds the normalized benchmark, participant splits and a fixed weighted candidate. It also verifies an openly licensed original Zenodo file and reports aggregate intake findings for 198 menstrual histories. No external forecast has yet been scored. Consult that follow-up for the updated source-access decisions; the candidate assessment below records the initial review.

## Evaluation protocol v1

This is the project's proposed evaluation contract. Freeze the final dataset-specific manifest, exclusions, candidate versions, metric definitions and acceptance thresholds **before examining held-out outcomes**. Independent clinical/editorial review and a suitable dataset are still needed before setting release criteria.

### Target and information available at the time

- Target: the next explicitly recorded period-start date, relative to the latest recorded start. It is not proof of an unobserved physiological event or ovulation. A long gap stays a long recorded interval unless its cause is independently known.
- Primary issuance: immediately after a start is logged. Separately evaluate fixed later landmarks; the synthetic harness uses 0, 14 and 35 elapsed days (cycle days 1, 15 and 36). A cycle already ended at a later landmark is outside that landmark's risk set. Publish those counts; do not combine changing denominators as an improvement in accuracy.
- Inputs: only records, context answers and corrections available by the issue time. Keep a fixed issued forecast for scoring even if the app later withholds an expired estimate. Score later forecasts separately; never replace an earlier miss with a later answer.
- The journal stores dated records but lacks a complete history of when each field was entered/corrected. A retrospective export cannot reconstruct genuine as-of knowledge. Call such a replay **idealized historical evaluation**, with an explicit same-day logging assumption. A future prospective study needs consented forecast/entry audit records; that would be a separately designed local feature, not new telemetry enabled by this work.
- Preserve absent outcomes. Dropout or study end does not mean an indefinitely long cycle or a correct prediction. Count forecasts with unknown outcomes in availability, separate them from accuracy, and report follow-up. Explicitly review missing-not-at-random bias.

### Methods and comparison fairness

Start with the existing fixed median policy and rounded mean, last-interval and fixed-28 comparators. Fixed 28 is a benchmark, never a fallback for missing records. Compare point methods on identical issued/scorable cases and publish coverage separately. The reference policy's median-based fit gate advantages neither a claim of universal superiority nor a fair test of every alternative's own deployment policy; evaluate each complete candidate policy separately before choosing it.

The next new candidate should be a prespecified recency-weighted robust estimator, tested for a tradeoff between adapting to drift and propagating a bad last entry. Select any weighting/window only on development participants. A hierarchical adherence-aware distribution is a later candidate when cohort access, implementation rights and representative training data are established. Do not tune any method on the synthetic examples to manufacture a winner.

Separate people into development, calibration and final test groups; any population priors or fitted parameters must exclude calibration/test participants as appropriate. Within a held-out person, personalization may use their earlier history only. Score chronologically, keeping all that person's cycles together in aggregate resampling. Track a distinct external cohort where possible. Do not exclude a test interval because its realized length is unusual, because the future label is inconvenient, or because later records reveal a condition not known at issuance.

### Measures to publish

| Question                          | Required reporting                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| How often is an answer available? | Offered / all eligible opportunities; reasons for withholding; cold-start delay; opportunities with and without known outcomes; people who never receive an answer. Also distinguish user opt-in from algorithmic eligibility.                                    |
| How wrong are issued dates?       | Mean absolute error, median and 90th-percentile absolute error, signed error (positive = predicted too late), and fractions within ±1, ±3 and ±7 days. Include the largest errors and sample counts.                                                              |
| Is a range useful?                | Observed coverage **and** width, at each specified nominal probability if probabilities are proposed. Historical min–max is descriptive and has no promised probability. Six intervals cannot justify a universal “90% confidence” label.                         |
| Who is represented?               | Results and availability by prespecified history length, variability, life stage, relevant hormone use, missingness, and device/source where applicable. Unknown context stays unknown. A withheld perimenopause cohort has unavailable accuracy, not zero error. |
| How certain is the evaluation?    | Participant-cluster uncertainty intervals and independent test counts; examine cohort and time shifts. Avoid treating repeated cycles as independent people. Calibration-set success must also hold in the untouched test group.                                  |

Existing six-interval, 365-day, 14–90-day, spread and fit cutoffs are preview product rules, not clinical definitions or evidence-based release thresholds. Do not change them solely to improve a headline MAE. Any calibrated range proposal needs separate review of coverage, width, subgroup failures and time dependence; neither a Bayesian label nor a residual-based interval automatically establishes calibration.

## Reproducible full-policy check

Run `pnpm research:predictions` (Windows: `./scripts/pnpm.ps1 --config.verify-deps-before-run=false run research:predictions`). It executes the actual `periodEstimate` function through [the research harness](../research/period-policy.ts), unlike the earlier candidate-only `pnpm validate:estimates` check. The [scenario script](../scripts/evaluate-period-policy.ts) prints all rules and three issuance tables. Fixtures are fictional, begin on January 1, 2025, and assume same-day logging and fixed context. The final start has 40 days of follow-up and no known next onset.

Day-1 results below include cold-start opportunities and the final unknown outcome. “Scored” counts only offered dates with a known next start. MAE is in days; `—` means unavailable.

| Synthetic case                | Offered / opportunities | Scored / observed outcomes | Median MAE | Inside historical spread |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------ |
| Stable 28                     | 7/13                    | 6/12                       | 0.00       | 6/6                      |
| Alternating 29/30             | 7/13                    | 6/12                       | 0.50       | 6/6                      |
| Gradual drift, 24 through 35  | 7/13                    | 6/12                       | 3.00       | 0/6                      |
| Abrupt change, 28 to 42       | 5/13                    | 4/12                       | 3.50       | 4/4                      |
| Six 28-day intervals, then 95 | 1/8                     | 1/7                        | 67.00      | 0/1                      |
| Repeated variable intervals   | 0/13                    | 0/12                       | —          | 0/0                      |
| Changing context              | 0/13                    | 0/12                       | —          | 0/0                      |

Findings from these deliberately constructed examples:

- A gradually lengthening history passes the rules while all six scored targets fall outside its historical spread. Last-interval MAE is 1 day on these same folds, versus median 3; that engineered trend is not population evidence favoring a replacement.
- Stable history cannot anticipate an unexpected 95-day recorded interval. Its 67-day error remains scored even though the result expires later. At the 35-day landmark the policy withholds that case as expired; that is a different availability result, not a repaired day-1 forecast.
- Cold starts, variable history, changing context, missing confirmation, disabled estimates, missed-cycle observations and positive tests have explicit unavailable states. No scored predictions means no measured accuracy.
- An unknown next onset contributes to availability when a forecast is offered, never a zero-error success. Final censored forecasts are also kept in the range-width denominator.

The seven new automated checks cover future-target/observation isolation, cold-start denominators, missing-outcome scoring, withholding, landmark expiry/follow-up boundaries, valid synthetic inputs and fixture immutability. These establish harness behavior, not clinical validity. Production date-boundary and policy tests remain in the existing suite. The harness is not a real-data importer or a simulation of delayed entry, corrections, uncertain onset labels or changing questionnaire answers.

## Development sequence and release gates

1. **Completed here:** evidence/access review, protocol, full-policy synthetic harness and regression checks. Keep the reference algorithm and experimental labeling.
2. **Follow-up completed:** [source provenance/intake and a normalized benchmark](prediction-benchmark.md), followed by [source-specific record preparation and a conditional analysis plan](prediction-record-preparation.md), now provide fixed participant groups and reproducible manifests. The comparison runner and uncertainty reporting remain next. Begin with period dates and explicitly label any idealized logging assumption. No personal health records in Git.
3. **Then:** compare the frozen baseline and candidate; measure error, availability and uncertainty on untouched people/time. Resolve the perimenopause evidence gap rather than removing the withholding rule to claim support.
4. **Before broader app release:** independent clinical/editorial assessment of intended use, wording and quantitative acceptance criteria; satisfactory external/prospective evidence; a reviewable UI proposal before any new prediction overlays or visual changes. Report failure or insufficient evidence as a valid research outcome.
5. **Separate biomarker track:** evaluate the measurement pathway and ovulation reference labels before implementation. Distinguish retrospective detection, prospective prediction and fertile-window estimation. Our existing Health Connect integration reads sleep only; adding temperature or other signals needs source availability, permission, measurement and device validation. Imported sleep alone receives no phase label from this research.

Typical hormone curves remain educational illustrations, not expected personal concentrations or a means of interpreting labs. Any future overlay needs source/assay/unit/timing review and clear measured-versus-illustrative labeling. Automatic clinical prompts and dosing advice are outside this research implementation. Local/offline operation remains the product direction; this pass adds no hosting dependency, cloud data collection, release-version bump or new APK.
